# DECLASAL (fictional) — Label extract, version 1.0

> Synthetic content for demonstration. Declasal is not a real medicine.

## Boxed Warning

WARNING: SERIOUS ADVERSE REACTIONS INCLUDING TENDINITIS, TENDON RUPTURE, PERIPHERAL NEUROPATHY,
CNS EFFECTS AND EXACERBATION OF MYASTHENIA GRAVIS

Declasal has been associated with disabling and potentially irreversible serious adverse reactions
that have occurred together, including tendinitis and tendon rupture, peripheral neuropathy, and
central nervous system effects. Discontinue Declasal immediately at the first sign of any such
reaction and avoid use in patients who have experienced any of these reactions.

Declasal may exacerbate muscle weakness in patients with myasthenia gravis. Avoid Declasal in
patients with a known history of myasthenia gravis.

Because Declasal has been associated with serious adverse reactions, reserve Declasal for patients
who have no alternative treatment options for the indication below.

## 1. Indications and Usage

DECLASAL is indicated in adults for the treatment of skin and skin-structure infections caused by
designated susceptible bacteria.

Limitations of use. The safety and effectiveness of DECLASAL have not been established in urinary
tract infection of any severity. DECLASAL has not been established in paediatric patients (under
18 years of age). DECLASAL has not been compared with any other antibacterial agent in a
head-to-head trial. Because of the serious adverse reactions in the Boxed Warning, reserve
DECLASAL for patients who have no alternative treatment options.

## 2. Dosage and Administration

The recommended dosage is 400 mg orally once daily for 10 days. A shorter course has not been
studied and is not recommended. Efficacy was assessed at the test-of-cure visit, Day 17 to Day 24.

## 3. Contraindications

DECLASAL is contraindicated in patients with known hypersensitivity to Declasal or any component
of the formulation.

## 4. Warnings and Precautions

Clostridioides difficile-associated diarrhea has been reported with DECLASAL. Evaluate patients who
present with diarrhea following therapy.

QT prolongation may occur with DECLASAL. Avoid DECLASAL in patients with known QT prolongation,
uncorrected hypokalaemia, or concurrent use of Class IA or Class III antiarrhythmic agents.

Hepatotoxicity, including fatal cases, has been reported with DECLASAL. Discontinue immediately if
signs of hepatitis occur.

Serious hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, have been reported.

Tendinitis and tendon rupture, peripheral neuropathy, central nervous system effects and
exacerbation of myasthenia gravis are described in the Boxed Warning.

## 5. Adverse Reactions

The most common adverse reactions (incidence 5% or greater) were nausea, diarrhea, headache,
dizziness and rash.

## 6. Clinical Studies

### 6.1 DCL-301 — clinical cure in mild cellulitis

The efficacy of DECLASAL was established in a single randomised, double-blind, active-controlled
trial of 412 adults aged 18 to 55 years with mild cellulitis. Patients received DECLASAL 400 mg
once daily for 10 days. Clinical cure was assessed at the test-of-cure visit, Day 17 to Day 24.

Clinical cure was achieved in 89.1% of patients in the modified intent-to-treat population and in
92.4% of patients in the clinically evaluable population.

### 6.2 Studies not conducted

DECLASAL has not been studied in complicated skin and skin-structure infection, in hospitalised
patients, in diabetic foot infection, in urinary tract infection, in patients over 55 years of age,
or in patients with significant comorbidities. No head-to-head trial against any other
antibacterial agent has been conducted. Time to clinical response before the Day 17 to Day 24
test-of-cure visit was not an endpoint in DCL-301 and has not been established.
