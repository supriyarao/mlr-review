# NEXAPRIL (fictional) — Label extract, version 2.1

> Synthetic content for demonstration. Nexapril is not a real medicine.

## 1. Indications and Usage

NEXAPRIL is indicated in adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus and established atherosclerotic
cardiovascular disease to reduce the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events.

Limitations of use. NEXAPRIL is not indicated for adults with type 2 diabetes who do not
have established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease. NEXAPRIL is not indicated for type 1
diabetes. The safety and effectiveness of NEXAPRIL have not been established in paediatric
patients. NEXAPRIL has not been compared with any other glucose-lowering therapy in a head-to-head
cardiovascular outcomes trial.

## 2. Dosage and Administration

The recommended starting dosage is 25 mg orally once daily, which may be increased to 50 mg
once daily after four weeks if additional glycaemic control is required and the starting dose is
tolerated.

Assess renal function before initiating treatment and periodically thereafter.

## 3. Contraindications

- Severe renal impairment (eGFR below 30 mL/min/1.73 m²) or end-stage renal disease.
- Known serious hypersensitivity to nexapril or any excipient.

## 4. Warnings and Precautions

Hypoglycaemia. The risk of hypoglycaemia is increased when NEXAPRIL is used with insulin or an
insulin secretagogue. Consider reducing the dose of the concomitant agent.

Volume depletion and hypotension. NEXAPRIL may cause intravascular volume contraction. Assess
volume status before initiating in patients taking diuretics or with impaired renal function.

Ketoacidosis. Cases of ketoacidosis, including with blood glucose below 250 mg/dL, have been
reported. Discontinue and evaluate promptly if ketoacidosis is suspected.

Genital mycotic infections. More frequent with NEXAPRIL than placebo; more common in patients
with a prior history.

Acute kidney injury. Consider temporarily discontinuing in settings of reduced oral intake or
fluid loss.

## 5. Adverse Reactions

Adverse reactions occurring in at least 2% of NEXAPRIL-treated patients and more frequently than
placebo in NEX-114:

| Adverse reaction | NEXAPRIL (n=4,706) | Placebo (n=4,706) |
|---|---|---|
| Nasopharyngitis | 8.9% | 8.1% |
| Urinary tract infection | 6.4% | 4.9% |
| Genital mycotic infection | 5.2% | 1.4% |
| Dizziness | 4.1% | 3.0% |
| Volume depletion | 3.6% | 2.2% |

## 6. Clinical Studies

### 6.1 NEX-114 — Major adverse cardiovascular events

Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled cardiovascular outcomes trial in 9,412 adults with
type 2 diabetes and established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease. Median follow-up 36
months. Comparator: placebo added to standard of care.

- Primary endpoint, three-point MACE (cardiovascular death, non-fatal myocardial infarction,
  non-fatal stroke): hazard ratio 0.78 (95% CI 0.69 to 0.88), a 22% relative risk reduction.
- Absolute event rates: 11.4% with NEXAPRIL versus 14.3% with placebo.

### 6.2 NEX-115 — Glycaemic control

Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 620 adults. Primary endpoint, change in
HbA1c from baseline at Week 24: −1.1% with NEXAPRIL versus −0.2% with placebo.

### 6.3 NEX-116 — Renal composite

Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 2,104 adults. Composite renal endpoint
over 36 months: hazard ratio 0.82 (95% CI 0.71 to 0.95).

### 6.4 Studies not conducted

No head-to-head cardiovascular outcomes trial against another glucose-lowering therapy has been
conducted. No study in adults without established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease has been
conducted. No paediatric study has been conducted.
